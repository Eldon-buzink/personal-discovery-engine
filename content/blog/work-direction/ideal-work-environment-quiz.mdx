"What's your ideal work environment" sounds like one question. Bearing scores it as three separate, independent measurements — because wanting structure, wanting independence, and wanting directness don't move together, and collapsing them into one label throws away the parts that don't happen to agree.

## Content vs. conditions

This is worth separating from [Direction](/blog/holland-code-riasec-explained) first, because the two get conflated constantly. Direction measures what kind of work content interests you — research, building, helping, leading. Working Style measures the conditions around doing that content — how much structure you want going in, how independently you prefer to operate, how directly you communicate along the way. Someone can rank Investigative as their top Direction interest and still want wildly different working conditions from another Investigative-ranked person — one wants a detailed research plan before starting, the other wants to improvise as questions come up. Same content interest, opposite environment.

## The three axes

### Structure — Structured ↔ Flexible
Whether you want a clear plan before starting, or do your best work improvising as you go. High-Structure environments (defined scope, known steps) energize one end; high-Flexible environments (loose scope, room to adapt) energize the other.

### Independence — Independent ↔ Collaborative
Whether you do your best thinking alone or by working through it with other people. This isn't about introversion or extraversion — it's specifically about how you solve problems, not how you recharge.

### Directness — Direct ↔ Diplomatic
Whether you'd rather say something blunt and honest, or choose your words to soften a hard message. This shapes what kind of team culture actually feels comfortable versus exhausting, independent of the work itself.

Bearing scores all three from 21 items — 7 per axis — as a continuous position rather than a forced label. There's no "you're a Structured person"; there's a specific point on the line, because most people aren't at either extreme, and the exact position is more informative than which half they're closer to.

## Why three separate positions instead of one environment type

A single "your ideal environment" type would have to compress three independent axes into one label, which only works cleanly for the minority of people who land at a similar extreme on all three. Most people are mixed — high-Structure but also high-Independent, for instance, wanting a clear plan but wanting to execute it alone rather than in constant collaboration. A combined type collapses that mix into something that isn't quite right on any axis. Three separate positions describe the actual combination instead of rounding it off into the nearest available type.
