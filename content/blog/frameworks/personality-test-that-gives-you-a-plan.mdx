Most personality tests are built to produce a result screen, and the result screen is where the product ends. You get a type, maybe a paragraph of description, and then it's on you to figure out what any of it means for the actual decision you were trying to make.

## What "just a label" leaves you with

A four-letter type or a single trait score is a stopping point, not a starting one. It describes you; it doesn't point anywhere. Figuring out what an INFJ result implies for whether to take a specific job offer, or what a high-Extraversion score implies for how to handle a specific relationship pattern, is left entirely to the person holding the result — the test measured something, but the bridge from measurement to decision doesn't exist in the product itself.

## What each of Bearing's branches actually gives you

Bearing's report is built around several independently scored branches, each of which is closer to a decision than a label:

- **Your Direction** ranks all six RIASEC interest types by how much the content of the work actually holds your attention — a direct input into "what kind of work should I be looking for," not just a personality descriptor.
- **Your Working Style** scores three independent conditions (Structure, Independence, Directness) — a direct input into "what kind of environment or team should I be looking for," separate from what the work itself is.
- **Your Energy** ranks what fuels and what drains you across three needs — a direct input into "why does this specific role feel exhausting even though I'm good at it," which a label alone can't diagnose.
- **Your Relationships** places you on two dimensions and names the resulting pattern — a direct input into "why do I keep ending up in the same relationship dynamic," not just a description of your general temperament.

None of these stop at "here's what you are." Each one is built to connect to a specific, real question a person is already asking.

## Why this needed more than one number

A single blended score can describe a person; it can't easily be cross-referenced against itself. Bearing's mid-range scoring already treats a facet result as a specific, situational finding rather than a vague middle — and the same design choice shows up at the branch level: keeping Direction, Working Style, Energy, and Relationships as separate measurements means they can be checked against each other (does your top interest match your preferred working conditions? does your role hit your top fuel category?), which is exactly the comparison a single fixed type has no mechanism for making.
