Someone in a meeting is looking out the window. Ask them what they were thinking about and it's rarely nothing — it's usually a half-formed version of some other scenario entirely, unrelated to the agenda. That drift is what this facet measures, and it has almost nothing to do with whether the person is any good at making art.

The four items behind Imagination in Bearing's assessment: whether you have a vivid imagination, whether you enjoy wild flights of fantasy, whether you love to daydream, and — reverse-scored — whether you seldom get lost in thought. All four are about the ease and frequency with which your mind generates something other than what's directly in front of you. None of them ask whether you do anything with what shows up.

That's the useful distinction, and it's easy to miss because "imaginative" and "creative" get used interchangeably in everyday speech. Bearing keeps them separate on purpose: Imagination is its own facet, and Artistic Interests — engagement with beauty, art, and aesthetic experience — is a different one entirely. You can be high in one and unremarkable in the other. A person who constantly generates hypotheticals but has no particular pull toward art, music, or design is high-Imagination, not high-Artistic-Interests. The reverse combination exists too: someone who is deeply moved by art but whose own mind doesn't wander much on its own.

## Where the wandering actually helps

In open-ended problem-solving, the same drift that looks unproductive in a meeting is often the mechanism generating the eventual solution — the mind trying out scenarios nobody explicitly asked for, some of which turn out to be relevant. Engineers debugging a stubborn issue and writers stuck on a plot problem report the same experience: the answer arrived while thinking about something else, not while staring directly at the problem.

## Where it costs you

The same trait is a liability in work that requires sustained, literal attention to one thing — proofreading a contract, following a checklist, monitoring a dashboard for an anomaly. The mind that generates useful tangents in open-ended work generates costly ones here; a wandering attention during a safety check isn't a feature.

## Reading it with the rest of your Openness facets

Imagination sits next to Artistic Interests, Emotionality, Adventurousness, Intellect, and Liberalism in Bearing's report. High Imagination and high Intellect together tend to produce someone who both generates unusual ideas and wants to examine them rigorously — a specific and useful combination that neither facet predicts alone.
