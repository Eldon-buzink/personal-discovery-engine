Bearing's assessment measures Trust with four items: whether you trust others, whether you believe others have good intentions, whether you think well of people, and — scored in reverse — whether you suspect hidden motives in others. Read as a group, they're really asking one question from four angles: when you don't have information about someone's intent, what do you assume?

The word "trust" makes the low end of this facet sound like a flaw — something to overcome. It's worth resisting that framing. Low Trust is a default assumption, not a character judgment, and defaults exist because they're useful often enough to keep around.

## What the low end is actually doing

A contractor quotes a repair at three times what a friend in the trade says it should cost. Someone high in Trust is more likely to take the number at face value, maybe push back once, and move on. Someone low in Trust asks for a second quote before agreeing to anything — not because they think every contractor is dishonest, but because their default assumption leaves more room for the possibility.

That second person will occasionally offend an honest contractor who really did quote fairly. They'll also, more often than the first person, avoid getting overcharged by the ones who didn't. Low Trust is a hedge against a real and unevenly distributed risk — the fact that some fraction of people you deal with genuinely are trying to get one over on you, and you don't know in advance which fraction that is.

## Where high Trust wins instead

The calculation flips in low-stakes, high-frequency interactions — the checkout clerk, the new coworker, the stranger asking for directions. Most of these encounters carry close to zero risk of bad intent, and treating every one of them with suspicion has its own cost: it's exhausting, and it makes you worse company. High Trust is calibrated correctly far more often than it's calibrated wrong, for the simple reason that most people, most of the time, are not trying to deceive you.

The trait isn't measuring who's right about people in general. It's measuring where you set the needle before you have specific information — and both settings are correct in the situations they were built for.

## Reading it with the rest of Agreeableness

Trust sits alongside Morality, Altruism, Cooperation, Modesty, and Sympathy in Bearing's report. Low Trust paired with high Morality is a common and coherent combination — skeptical of others' motives, but not willing to exploit anyone else's. Seeing all six together is what makes the pattern legible instead of just one number.
